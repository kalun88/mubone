The first six moves

1Let it hear you

Mic button, top right. Set the paint gate in Settings → Audio so the room doesn't paint. If you'll loop, press Measure under Latency once — with the speakers reaching the mic.

2Aim

The mouse turns the sphere; the cursor is the centre. Over any panel the sphere stops. ⌥ freezes the cursor and gives you the pointer; ⌥ again takes it back.

3Paint

Hold space and play. The tool in hand — dots to start — lays a mark for each moment. Let go; they stay.

4Listen back

Pass the cursor over the marks: grains from whatever is in reach. [ ] set the reach. S caps the cursor — silence until you uncap.

5Play a phrase whole

2play, and again — it loops at once, pinned. 1play, and again — a line: a take that fires whole when the cursor touches it. The keys under the tiles are the factory ones.

6Pin and mix

↓pins what the cursor is sounding. ⇧Tab opens the mixer — a fader per pin. ↑unpins the top one. ⌘S saves the piece; nothing saves itself.

Scratch, and pinned

Everything on the sphere is one or the other. Scratch sounds only under the cursor. Pinned plays on its own.

Scratch is what the cursor reads

Everything you paint lands here. It is silent until the cursor is on it: grain marks in reach are granulated, a line fires when touched. The cursor reads it, the eraser edits it, S caps it, sweep clears it.

Pinned plays on its own

A loop or a cloud. It has a track in the mixer, feeds the house, and keeps going wherever the cursor is. Sweep leaves it alone. Unpin is what stops it — and the material is scratch again, back under the lens.

The pin is the line between them

A pinned cloud owns its marks: the cursor will not granulate inside it, so pinning never doubles a sound — muted or not. A pinned loop's stroke is still a line: touch it and it fires on top of the loop.

Three ways across

Now — ↓pins what the cursor is sounding, where you stand. On release — a tile wearing the pin sticker pins its own stroke the moment you let go: a tape tool as a loop, a grain tool as a moving cloud. While you paint — hold ↓during a tape stroke and it loops now and keeps growing until you let go.

The palette

The big tile is the hand

What space and a click on the sphere play. Click any tool — in the rail or on the strip — to take it in hand. The verb comes with the tool: a strip tile brings its own, a rail row brings its engine's (tape latches, grain and erase are held). Right-click the hand tile to flip it.

The rest are quick access

Each fires its own tool with its own key, printed under it, without touching the hand — granulate with space while 2drops a loop. Move a tile and its key goes with it.

Build it by dragging

Drag a row in from the left rail to place it. Drag a tile along to move it, off to remove it. Nine is full.

The three stickers

1 —
A tile can wear three. Top-left the drop, top-right the pin, bottom-left the key. Each is half off the tile, on its own disc.

The drop — wet or dry

On every grain tile. Outlined is dry, filled is wet. Tap it to flip; it never takes the tool in hand. Tape, erase and lens tiles have none: only grain paint can be wet.

The pin — pins on end

Shows when the tile pins its stroke the moment you let go: a tape tool as a loop, a grain tool as a moving cloud. On the strip it is a mark, not a button. Flip it on the tool's row in the left rail — the pin beside its name — or the on end line in its drawer. Dub wears it always: its take can only go into a pinned loop.

The key — its binding

The key, button or note that fires this position. Click it, press the new one. Right-click clears it to a dash; the dash is still the cell, click it to bind again. A gesture rides inside the sticker — tap, long, ×2. The hand's own sticker is the spacebar, and click.

Wet paint

Dry: a stroke keeps the sound it was painted with

Turn the knobs afterwards and only the next stroke changes. What you laid down an hour ago sounds as it did an hour ago. Every brush ships dry except the pen.

Wet: every stroke this brush painted follows its knobs

Turn the pitch and the whole wash moves, wherever the cursor is — including inside a pinned cloud it painted. Only the sound moves: the marks never leave where they landed.

Drying freezes them where they sound

Tap the drop off and the strokes keep the sound they have at that moment, for good. Tap it on again and only what you paint from now on is wet — the old strokes stay dry.

Where the switch is

The drop on the tile, the same drop on the tool's row in the left rail, and the head of its drawer. All three are the one switch. While a wet brush is in hand its marks wear a ring in its hue.

The drawer

Every tool has one. It is where its sound is set, and it opens from the tool rail.

1 · Tab shows the tool rail

Every tool, grouped by engine, down the left. Click one to take it in hand. Tab again hides it.

2 · Click the door on its row

The at the row's right edge. The drawer opens beside the rail with that tool's name at its head. Click the door again to close it, or esc — once for the drawer, twice for the rail.

3 · What's in it

Every parameter of that tool, one line each. Drag a slider, or type into its number. Double-click a slider and it goes back to its tick. A switch is a yes or no — wet and cloud on end are the two you will use most. What you set here is that tool's sound from now on.

The + and the DEL

The + beside an engine's name in the rail mints a new tool from the sliders as they are, so a sound you like becomes its own tile. DEL at the head of a drawer deletes that tool — a factory one comes back with Settings → Reset.

The row's own two buttons

The drop and the pin on a row are switches you can tap without opening anything: wet or dry, pins on end or not. The same two stickers the palette tile wears.

The cursor decides what a pin is

Pin while on a stroke and it becomes a loop. Pin while granulating and the marks in reach become a cloud that keeps granulating there. Pin while painting a tape stroke and the loop grows until you let go. A tile can also pin for you on release — the pin sticker, above.

Unpin takes the top track

The selected frame holds it, and the frame is always there — empty, it says so. sort says which is on top — nearest to the cursor, farthest, or oldest. old holds still while you play.

Follow

follow on: the faders follow the cursor — nearest loudest, the rest handing over by distance — and their edges turn violet to say so. Off, every pin plays at full. curve is how gradual the handover is.

Press a number for its in / out

Each pin's own ramps. in: pin and unmute. out: unpin and mute. Ten seconds on both and a cloud swells and dies slowly every time.

Under the tracks

clouds and loops, each with its own M and S. Then unpin all and mute. Pin and unpin are on the palette, on their own keys; a track's own M and S clear one at a time.

Saving

The piece is the music

Everything on the sphere and the sound it was played on — takes, marks, pins, loops, lines, their audio — in one .mubone file. ⌘S saves it. The first time it asks where; after that it's silent. Its name sits beside the version in the top bar, with a dot while something is unsaved.

Save As

⇧⌘S always asks, and that file is the piece from then on. Use it to keep a version before you take a set somewhere else.

Open, and New

⌘O opens a piece and replaces what is on the sphere — save first. File → Open Recent holds the last eight; a double-clicked .mubone opens in mubone. ⌘N is an empty piece. An opened piece plays: its loops and clouds come back sounding.

Nothing saves itself

There is no autosave. Closing the window with unsaved work asks once; that prompt is the only net.

What the app remembers on its own

The rig: your settings, every tool's drawer, the palette and its keys, bindings, the sensor calibration you saved, the window layout. All of it survives quitting and relaunching without you doing anything. It is not in the piece — open someone else's piece and your rig stays yours.

Export

Settings → Export · Import → Export writes that rig to a setup file you can carry to another machine or keep as a backup — devices, calibration, bindings, the palette, the layout. No music in it.

Import

Reads a setup file back and merges it into what is here rather than replacing it: what the file names changes, what it doesn't leaves alone.

Reset

Settings → Reset. Reset all puts every remembered setting back to factory, two clicks. Reset selected clears only the categories you switch on. Neither touches a saved piece or setup file.

The browser demo forgets

mubone.org/sim starts from factory on every new build. Only the Electron app keeps a rig.

Good to know

The sensor forgets on every boot

Two gestures before a set: the mount calibration (two poses, Settings → Sensors) and zero (`, facing the audience). Neither survives a power cycle.

No autosave

Nothing is written unless you ⌘S. The prompt when you close the window is the only net. The piece is the music; the rig — devices, keys, the palette — is a separate export.

Measure latency once per interface

Settings → Audio → Latency → Measure, with the output reaching the mic. Loops land in time because of this number; it's remembered per device pair.

The paint gate gates painting, not sound

Below the line on the gate meter nothing is painted. It never attenuates. Set it above the room and below your quietest playing.

Sweep vs erase all

Sweep (the broom) clears only what isn't pinned — pins keep sounding, and it frees recording memory. Erase all takes everything, pins included.

Recording memory fills

The bar in the top bar. At full, a new take is refused until you sweep. Watch it in a long set.

A mute is a DJ mute

The loop keeps running silently. Unmute and it's where it would have been — not at the top. Unpin is the thing that stops it.

A tape's speed, pitch and direction are frozen when you draw it

speed, pitch and reverse in a tape tool's drawer. Speed is tape — the pitch follows it. Pitch is a shift on top that leaves the length alone. step snaps both to semitones, or to octaves and fifths. Moving them changes the NEXT stroke, never one already on the sphere.

Arriving at the tail flips it

With the lens on start: ends, entering a stroke at its back plays it backwards — and on a reversed tape, entering at the back plays it forwards. Approach is the control.

Stroke mode — a grain stroke plays itself

Set the cursor's mode to stroke. The cursor stops granulating on its own; touch a grain stroke and a reader launches from where you touched, retracing the stroke at the pace you painted it. order still chooses random or step inside it. Pin while it runs and it becomes a cloud on that path.

on dwell: grain — it plays, then opens

A take plays through once, and only then does the cursor start granulating its material — until you move away. The same in stroke mode: the reader walks the stroke, then hands it to the cursor.

Trail stays in the room

The trail tool pins itself as a cloud on the path you drew the moment you let go. Any grain tool can, with cloud on end in its drawer.

Dub goes into the nearest loop

3play, release: the take joins the nearest pinned loop as a layer at the phase you played it, every cycle. Longer than the loop and it stacks. Nothing pinned, the first take becomes the loop.

Dub decay — the loop forgets

decay in the dub's drawer, a percentage. While you dub, everything already in the loop steps down by it at every pass, so the loop is always the last few passes. Nothing fades in playback. At 0 the layers stack for ever.

Dub as a one-shot

Set the dub tile's verb to bang (right-click it on the strip): one press records exactly one cycle of the loop and lets go by itself. Toggle is the long overdub, momentary is a punch-in.

The eraser is the editor

Erase a line's tail to shorten it, its head to trim, its middle to split it in two. Hold an eraser 8 s for erase all.

One tool plays at a time

While one runs, the other tool keys wait; its own key is what ends it. Pin and unpin always work.

The browser demo is a demo

mubone.org/sim has no sensor and stereo only, and forgets everything on a new build. The instrument is the Electron app.

Glossary

markone moment of what you played, painted where the cursor was. Size is loudness; a stroke is a run of them.
scratcheverything painted and not pinned — silent until the cursor is on it. What the lens reads, what sweep clears.
pinneda loop or a cloud playing on its own, with a track in the mixer. Unpin and it is scratch again.
the handthe one tool space plays.
enginewhat kind of tool: source · cursor · tape · grain · erase. One colour each, everywhere.
tapea take played whole — line, slice, loop, dub.
graina take played in fragments, read from the marks in reach.
cursorhow the cursor reads. The CURSOR section at the foot of the tool rail holds every setting: scope (grains, tape or both), radius and its fade, mode — radius (everything in reach) or nearest (the closest) — then, for grains, depth, all, k and step. Its tile on the strip turns the eye off and on.
reachhow far the cursor reads, and how far the eraser and a line's trigger zone extend.
linea take that fires whole when the cursor touches its stroke.
pinmaterial that keeps playing on its own: a loop, or a cloud.
on endwhat a stroke becomes when you let go: scratch, or pinned — a loop, a cloud. The pin sticker says which.
weta grain brush whose knobs keep moving every stroke it painted. Dry, a stroke is frozen as painted. The drop on the tile.
stickera mark on a tile's corner: the drop, the pin, the key.
clouda patch of marks granulating where it is. A moving cloud loops the path you drew.
busall the clouds, or all the loops, with one mute and one solo.
capthe cursor turned off. S.
dryyour live input passed straight to the house, spatialised, beside what the cursor makes of it.
house · monitorthe audience's mix and yours. Pins feed the house; the cursor feeds your monitor, and the house unless capped.
zerowhich way is forward. Face the audience, `.
